Strict Video Quality Rules:

1. Human-like AI Avatar + Natural Voice:
- The avatar must speak the explanation naturally (not robotic).
- Use conversational teaching language, pauses, and emphasis like a real teacher.
- Match facial expression and tone to the content (smiling when encouraging, focused when explaining complex ideas).

2. Rich Visual Experience (Very Important):
- Do NOT just put a talking avatar in front of plain text.
- Every segment should include relevant visual support:
  - On-screen key points / bullet points
  - Diagrams and illustrations
  - Mathematical equations (step-by-step)
  - Code snippets with highlighting
  - Graphs, timelines, flowcharts, process animations
  - Relevant images or conceptual visualizations
- Visuals must change and appear at the right moment to support what the avatar is saying.

3. Subject-Aware Visuals:
- Mathematics → Step-by-step equations, graphs
- Physics → Diagrams, force illustrations, process flows
- Biology → Labeled diagrams, cycle illustrations
- History → Timelines, maps
- Programming → Code + output + architecture diagrams
- General → Clean conceptual illustrations and examples

4. Overall Experience Goal:
Create the feeling that the student is attending a live, well-prepared lesson conducted by a skilled human teacher — not watching a robotic avatar reading a script.

5. Output Requirements:
- Break the lesson into logical short segments (20–60 seconds each recommended).
- Keep spoken text natural and spoken-friendly (not written essay style).
- Clearly describe every visual so that video generation tools (HeyGen, D-ID, Synthesia, or custom pipeline) can render them properly.
